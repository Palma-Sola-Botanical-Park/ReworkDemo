Tree Crinum
Crinum asiaticum Amaryllidaceae
🌍 Non-native✅ Not Invasive☠️ Toxic — Keep Away
⚡Quick Hits
  • One of the largest true bulbs in the plant kingdom — a single underground bulb can weigh up to 40 pounds.
  • Flowers bloom at sunset, releasing sweet fragrance engineered to guide nocturnal Sphinx moths.
  • Nicknamed cemetery lilies — virtually immortal plants that survive freezes, fires, and decades of neglect.
  • Thrives in two opposite extremes: bone-dry sand or fully submerged in standing water.
🌍Origin

Native to tropical Asia, ranging from India and Sri Lanka through Southeast Asia to parts of northern Australia and many Pacific islands. Widely introduced and naturalized throughout tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, including coastal Florida.

🔍More Information
  • The flowers glow under moonlight and intensify their fragrance at dusk — a precisely timed system designed exclusively for Sphinx moths, their primary pollinators.
  • A striking purple-leafed cultivar named Queen Emma honors the 19th-century humanitarian queen consort of Hawaii, who grew them in her royal gardens.
  • Ancient cultures across Asia and the Pacific used the plant medicinally — to treat skin ailments, soothe jellyfish stings, and even stun fish for easier harvesting.
  • Unlike most bulbs that rot in wet conditions, this plant's underground chemistry allows it to survive flooding that would kill virtually any other bulb plant.
🦋Wildlife Value

Flowers are engineered for Sphinx moths — the fragrance intensifies specifically at dusk to guide these primary pollinators. Dense clumping foliage shelters lizards and small animals at ground level. Heavy fruit drops attract foraging wildlife at the base. While toxic to mammals, it supports a nocturnal pollinator community that few other park plants can match.

🔬Reproduction & Identification
Offsets (Pups)

Mother bulbs continuously produce underground offshoots, forming dense spreading colonies over time.

Seeds

Sphinx moth-pollinated flowers produce heavy, fleshy seeds that drop and sprout directly on the soil surface.

Flowers

Tall leafless stalks bear clusters of highly fragrant white or pinkish spidery blooms with long purple stamens.

Fruit & Seeds

Fleshy, lumpy, irregular green pods containing unusually large soft green-to-tan seeds that germinate readily.

What to Look For

The tall flower stalks rising above the foliage at dusk, the distinctive spider-like white blooms, and the massive clumping base. Fragrance is strongest at sunset — the best time to visit this plant.

⚠️Edibility & Toxicity

Not edible. Contains lycorine and other alkaloids causing vomiting, low blood pressure, and abdominal pain. Seek immediate medical care if any part is ingested. Keep away from children and pets.
