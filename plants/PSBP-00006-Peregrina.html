Peregrina
Jatropha integerrima Euphorbiaceae
🌍 Non-native✅ Not Invasive☠️ Toxic — All Parts
⚡Quick Hits
  • Every single part is poisonous — yet it's one of the most widely planted ornamentals in South Florida.
  • In frost-free conditions it blooms every month of the year — one of the longest-flowering plants in any Florida garden.
  • Look closely: the same branch carries three completely different leaf shapes — oval, fiddle-shaped, and three-lobed — simultaneously.
  • Its seeds contain curcin, a toxin in the same class as ricin, though less potent.
🌍Origin

Native to Cuba and Hispaniola in the West Indies.

🔍More Information
  • The botanist who named it integerrima — Latin for 'most whole and unlobed' — apparently examined it on a day when only the simple oval leaves were visible.
  • It belongs to the same family as the Poinsettia, Crown of Thorns, and the Pencil Tree also growing at this park — the Euphorbiaceae, connecting some of the most beautiful and most dangerous plants in the tropical world.
  • Its cousin Jatropha curcas has been intensively researched as a biodiesel feedstock — its seeds produce oil that can power diesel engines directly, making this genus an unusual intersection of ornamental gardening and renewable energy.
  • Despite its toxicity, the sap has been used in traditional Caribbean medicine — a reminder that the line between poison and remedy is often a matter of dose and preparation.
🦋Wildlife Value

One of the most reliable butterfly and hummingbird plants in South Florida, blooming every month of the year. The year-round scarlet flower clusters are particularly valuable in winter when most other nectar sources have shut down — filling a gap that few other ornamental plants can. Documented to attract a wide range of butterfly species as well as ruby-throated hummingbirds.

🔬Reproduction & Identification
Reproduction

By seed and stem cuttings. Seeds viable but not invasive in Florida. Blooms on current year's growth — can be pruned anytime without sacrificing flowers.

Flowers

Brilliant scarlet five-petaled blooms about one inch across in multi-flowered terminal clusters, appearing nearly every month of the year.

Fruit

Small round capsules turning from green to brown at maturity, containing smooth, speckled, toxic seeds.

Leaves

Highly variable — the same branch carries simple ovals, fiddle-shaped leaves pinched at the middle, and three-lobed leaves simultaneously. Bronze when young, glossy dark green at maturity.

Sap

Milky white latex sap released when stems are cut or damaged. Irritating to skin, eyes, and mucous membranes — wear gloves when pruning.

What to Look For

The nearly constant scarlet flower clusters are the signature feature. Look for the leaf shape game: three completely different leaf forms on the same branch. Watch for butterfly and hummingbird visitors throughout the year.

☠️Edibility & Toxicity

Do not eat any part of this plant. All parts are toxic — particularly the seeds, which contain curcin, a toxin in the same class as ricin. Milky latex sap irritates skin, eyes, and mucous membranes. Wear gloves when pruning. Keep away from children and pets.
