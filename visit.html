Find Us

Getting here

We're tucked into the northeast shore of Palma Sola Bay, just west of Manatee Avenue. Free parking. Paved and natural paths suitable for most visitors.

Accessibility

Main paths are paved or packed gravel. Some garden areas have natural ground cover. Wheelchairs and strollers can navigate most of the park. Call ahead if you have specific questions.

🐾 Pets Welcome

Well-behaved pets on leash are welcome. Please clean up. Keep pets from disturbing plant life or wildlife. Note: several plants in the collection are toxic to dogs — see the plant library and filter by "Toxic."

Rare Fruit Trail

Our warm little microclimate

Palma Sola sits in a notably frost-free pocket — which means we can grow things that shouldn't survive in Manatee County. The Manatee Rare Fruit Council has tended an exotic fruit section here since 2008.

Star fruit, dragon fruit, jackfruit, lychee, sapodilla, tamarind, Surinam cherry, sugar apple, and more. New specimens are added every year, often donated by the Rare Fruit Council.

Hidden Garden Nursery

Plants grown right here

Tucked near the west entrance, our volunteer-run nursery carries plants propagated directly from the collection. Reasonable prices. 100% of sales benefit the park.

Looking for something specific? Call ahead. Volunteers know exactly what's rooted and ready — including Florida natives you won't find at a box store.

Nursery Hours

October through May
Tuesdays & Saturdays · 9am – Noon
100% volunteer-run · All proceeds to the park

Photography

Personal photography is welcome and encouraged — especially if you're going to submit observations to iNaturalist. Tag us on social media and help spread the word about the park.

Group Visits

Bring your group

School groups, garden clubs, senior programs, and community organizations are welcome. For groups of 10 or more, we ask that you call ahead so we can accommodate everyone.
